Crop water demand · climate change · regional water-resource planning

Agricultural water demand connects climate, crop physiology, land management, and regional water availability. Our research combines crop-specific water-balance calculations with multi-model climate projections to identify where irrigation requirements are likely to grow and which crops will place the greatest pressure on future supplies.

Featured Research

Haoxuan Yang · Agricultural Water Management · 2026

Increasing irrigation water requirements across croplands in the Contiguous United States throughout the 21st century

Haoxuan Yang et al. · Volume 323, Article 110044 · DOI: 10.1016/j.agwat.2025.110044

Using five CMIP6 global climate models, two emissions pathways (SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5), and ten major crop types, the study estimates how warming and changing atmospheric demand will reshape crop irrigation requirements through 2100.

  • National increase: late-century average irrigation requirements reach 404.4 mm yr⁻¹ under SSP2-4.5 (+10.3%) and 462.7 mm yr⁻¹ under SSP5-8.5 (+26.4%).
  • Temperature sensitivity: each 1°C of warming raises modeled irrigation requirements by approximately 4.2–5.0%.
  • Regional contrast: the Southern Great Plains and Midwest emerge as major total-demand hotspots, while the Southwest retains the highest requirement per unit cropland area.
  • Crop priorities: rice, alfalfa, and cotton are among the crops with especially high irrigation requirements.

Why It Matters

The projections turn broad climate scenarios into crop- and region-specific water information. This supports adaptation planning by showing where efficiency gains, crop choices, infrastructure, and groundwater or surface-water management may become most consequential.

Research Approach

  • Ensemble climate projections to represent model and scenario uncertainty.
  • Crop-specific irrigation-demand calculations across contiguous U.S. croplands.
  • Spatial comparison of total demand, per-area requirements, and warming sensitivity.
