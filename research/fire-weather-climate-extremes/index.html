Climate Change and Ecosystem Dynamics

Climate projections · extreme fire weather · landscape burn probability

Climate change can alter wildfire hazard through several pathways at once. Our work links Earth system model projections with fire-danger and fire-spread models to distinguish changes in the frequency of extreme fire weather from changes in the probability that a fire burns across the landscape.

Featured Research

Quan Zhang · Agricultural and Forest Meteorology · 2026

Different meteorological drivers govern future extreme fire weather frequency and burn probability in the Southern Great Plains

Quan Zhang, Jia Yang, Shanmin Fang, Alex Desjardins, Haoxuan Yang, Xiaohao Jiao, and Chris Zou · Volume 390, Article 111458 · DOI: 10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111458

The study combines projections from six Earth System Models with the Burning Index and FARSITE simulations. Historical conditions (2000–2019) are compared with mid-century (2040–2059) and late-century (2080–2099) periods under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 for four Southern Great Plains study areas.

  • More extreme days: warming and atmospheric drying consistently increase the future frequency of extreme fire weather.
  • Different drivers: thermodynamic changes govern how often extreme conditions occur, while aerodynamic conditions more directly regulate simulated fire spread.
  • Wind matters most for area burned: wind speed has the strongest partial association with burn probability (partial r = 0.70; partial R² = 48.5%).

Why It Matters

The results show that a single fire-weather indicator cannot fully describe future wildfire hazard. Planning benefits from tracking both thermodynamic stress—which increases the number of extreme days—and wind-sensitive spread potential, which can control the extent of individual fire events.

Research Approach

  • Multi-model climate ensembles and scenario-based uncertainty analysis.
  • Fire-danger diagnostics paired with spatial fire-spread simulation.
  • Attribution of changes to temperature, humidity, precipitation, and wind.
